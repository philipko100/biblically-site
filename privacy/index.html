Privacy Policy

Last updated 3 September 2026

This policy explains what the Biblically app stores, the legal basis for processing it, who it is shared with, and how to delete all of it. Two things shape everything below: we never sell, license, or share your personal information for anyone else's purposes, and there is no advertising and no cross-app tracking. The App does measure how it is used, so that it can be improved — you can turn that off, and Usage analytics says exactly what it involves.

About this policy

It covers the Biblically mobile app and the biblically.app website (together, the "App"). Biblically is made and run by an independent individual developer, not a company. Full details of who is responsible for your data, and how to get in touch, are set out further down.

"Personal information" means information relating to an identified or identifiable person, as defined by Canada's Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) and British Columbia's Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA), the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), the UK Data Protection Act 2018, the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA/CPRA), the Virginia Consumer Data Protection Act (VCDPA), and other applicable privacy laws. Which parts of this policy apply to you depends on where you live; the sections on your rights say so explicitly.

Biblically is for devotional and informational use only. It does not provide medical, psychological, legal, or pastoral counselling. The daily check-in records how you say you feel so that you can see your own patterns over time; it is not a clinical assessment and nobody reviews it. If you are struggling, please reach out to a qualified counsellor, licensed medical professional, or trusted pastoral caregiver. In an emergency, contact your local emergency services immediately — 911 in the United States, 112 across Europe.

Special category data, and why we ask for consent

Information revealing religious beliefs is a "special category" of personal data under the GDPR (Article 9) and receives extra protection. We process it on the basis of your explicit consent, given when you choose a tradition and answer the opening questions, and we process it solely to operate the App for you — showing the right canon, saving your journal, and displaying your own history back to you.

Your consent also covers the limited use of this information in usage analytics — your tradition setting, and what your reading reveals about your practice. That is a separate purpose from running the App, which is why it has its own off switch under Me → Privacy; turning it off stops that use immediately and costs you no functionality.

We do not use it for advertising or for profiling you as an individual. We do not sell it, license it, or share it with data brokers or AI developers. You can withdraw your consent for the App itself at any time by deleting your account, which erases the underlying data outright.

What we do not collect

We do not collect your location, contacts, photos, health data, calendar, or advertising identifier. We show no ads, build no advertising profiles, and do not track you across other apps or websites. The website sets no cookies and has no tracking pixels, which is why you were not asked to consent to any. The App does collect usage analytics, described next.

Usage analytics

To see which parts of Biblically work and which do not, the App records what people do in it, using PostHog as our analytics provider. This is on by default. You can turn it off at any time in the App under Me → Privacy → "Share anonymous usage data", and nothing further is sent from that moment.

What analytics records

  • Which screens you open and in what order, including which book and chapter you are reading and which passages you ask to have explained
  • That you completed the opening questions, signed in, saved a check-in, saved a journal entry, chose a tradition, turned Daily Focus on or off, saw the paywall, or deleted your account
  • Your daily check-in ratings, and the word count of a journal entry
  • Your tradition setting — Catholic, Orthodox, or neither
  • Standard app events (installed, updated, opened, backgrounded), your device type, operating system version and language, and which sign-in provider you used
  • A random account identifier, so these events can be grouped as one person's use over time

What analytics never records

The words you write are never sent. Not your prayer journal, not your notes, not your highlights. A journal entry is counted, not read — analytics sees "412 words", never the 412 words.

Your email address and name are never sent to analytics, and neither are your answers to the opening questions. The identifier used is the random one your account already has, not anything that names you.

The part that needs your consent

Your tradition reveals a religious belief, which is special category data (see below). Two of the items above touch it: the tradition setting itself, and the fact that reading a particular passage says something about your practice. We include them because knowing which canon people choose, and which parts of Scripture go unread, is how the App gets better at its actual job.

We rely on your consent for this, and consent you cannot withdraw would not be worth much — which is what the switch in the App is for. Turning analytics off stops all of it, and does not limit any feature.

Daily Focus and Screen Time

Daily Focus shields distracting apps until you have read. It is built on Apple's Screen Time (Family Controls) framework, and Apple's design means the App cannot see which apps you chose — the system returns an opaque reference, not a list. That reference is stored only on your iPhone, in the App's private storage. It is never transmitted to us or to anyone else, and we could not read it if it were. Nothing about your Screen Time usage reaches us.

Where your information is held, and international transfers

We are based in British Columbia, and our hosting and our analytics provider are both in the United States — so wherever you use the App from, your information is stored and processed in the US by Supabase and, unless you have turned analytics off, by PostHog.

While it is held there it is subject to United States law, and US courts, law enforcement and national security authorities may be entitled to obtain access to it under that law. We are telling you this plainly because Canadian privacy law expects an organisation transferring personal information outside the country to say so.

Where the GDPR or UK Data Protection Act applies, these transfers are made under the safeguards those laws require, including Standard Contractual Clauses entered into by our hosting provider.

How long we keep it

Your account and everything in it are kept for as long as you use the App. When you delete your account, that content is erased immediately — it is not queued, held for a grace period, or kept in a reduced form. Residual copies may persist briefly in our hosting provider's encrypted backups before those are overwritten in the normal course, typically within 30 days.

Two things sit outside your account and so are not affected by deleting it. Support emails you have sent us, and our replies, are kept for as long as needed to resolve your question and a reasonable period afterwards. Explanation text generated for a passage is cached without any link to the person who requested it; it is not personal information and it remains after an account is deleted.

We would retain information beyond that only where the law specifically requires it, or where it is needed to establish, exercise or defend a legal claim — the narrow exceptions the GDPR's right to erasure allows for. Nothing else survives deletion: your journal entries, highlights, notes, bookmarks, favourites, check-in answers and tradition setting are destroyed with the account and cannot be recovered by us or by you.

Deleting your account

In the App, go to Me → Account and tap Delete account. Deletion is immediate and permanent: your account and every record described above are removed from our database, and the copy held on your device is erased at the same time. There is no recovery period and we keep no separate copy of your content. You do not need to contact us or give a reason.

Signing out, by contrast, leaves your data intact so that you can sign back in.

Security

Connections between the App and our servers use TLS encryption, and data is encrypted at rest by our hosting provider. Access to the database is restricted per-user by row-level security, so one account cannot read another's content. No system is perfectly secure, but we take reasonable and appropriate technical and organisational measures to protect your information.

Children

Biblically is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. If you are under the age at which you can consent to data processing in your country — 13 to 16 depending on where you live in the EU — please use the App only with the permission of a parent or guardian. If you believe a child has provided us with personal information, contact us and we will delete the account.
